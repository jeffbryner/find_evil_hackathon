In the slack yesterday I made a flippant remark about receiving the message before it was sent. I was only half kidding. Let me explain why and what this is likely going to mean for FTL comms.

 

A common notion for quantum theory is that there are multiple universes that divide every time a decision is made. For example, there is a universe where I skipped breakfast (u1) this morning and one where I did not (u2). Now, the two universes divide at that one point and the consequences of those actions impact other things, making the two universes more disparate as the time goes on. Most universes are separated by minor decisions, so the u1 and u2 are almost identical and will likely never deviate far from each other. After all, what the eternal consequence of me skipping breakfast, right?

 

Well, let's say that I decide to skip breakfast every day from then on. Over time the DumDum in u1 may lose weight, make a pledge to eat healthier and work out more. This may make him attractive to a potential partner and he then makes decisions based on things directly associated with finding and obtaining a mate. While the DumDum in u2 continues on the same path he always has. After several months or years, the difference between u1 and u2 has become significant, not just in my own life, but in the lives of those I associate with.

 

To make things more complicated, quantum particles entangle with each other. This is what makes our FTL comms possible, but it is also where we run into issues. Entanglement means that I can send a message across the universe in real-time because the entangled quantum particles vibrate at the same frequency. But the issues we're seeing now is that our comms aren't just entangling in u1, they are also entangling in u2, u3, u4, etc. There is cross-universe entanglement.

 

Why does this matter? Well, I conducted a test that is like sending a ping on a computer. A ping tells you the latency between two points. The result of the ping was strange. When I ran the test the latency wasn't noticeable, which is amazing. However, as I checked the logs, I noticed that the response came in a fraction of a second before I sent the request. This means that someone or something sent a reply to a request that didn't yet exist.

 

I did some further experimentation. I set up a short comms link between two points and recorded my voice at this end, and the same on the receiving end. When I compared the recordings, they were nearly exactly the same. Note that I said nearly. On the receiving end there was an inflection on one of the words that didn't exist on the recording from the sending end. I've now run this test hundreds of times and each time the recordings are 99.999999973% (rounded up) accurate. In addition, the logs show that some of these comms occur a fraction of a second before they are sent, sometimes a fraction of a second after.

 

My theory is that the quantum communications sent by DumDum from u1 aren't actually delivered to u1. Instead they go to uY. Likewise, the comms we receive are from uQ. This means that while the comms are almost exactly the same, there is a 0.000000027 variance overall. The problem is that the comms we receive are not the comms we sent. We are receiving the "same" comms from ourselves in another universe. Now, this likely won't matter in most instances, but when if that variance is the difference between "shoot" and "don't shoot", or "cut the red wired" as opposed to "cut the green wire." Moreover, the time variation between universes means that we might receive comms for something that hasn't happened yet, or we might receive comms too late to act on. For example, if Nick's car didn't start this morning in u2, but he arrived for work on time in u1, he might give an order in u1 ten minutes earlier than we would receive it from u2.

 

Can you see what I'm saying here? If we can't force the entanglement with quantum particles in our own universe, we might, someday, see such an eventuality. The thing is, I don't know if this is a fixable thing. We might have to live with it and hope that the other "us" in the other universes are as good as we are.
